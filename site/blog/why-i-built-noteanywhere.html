Why I built NoteAnywhere

Notes that come back when you do.

I learn from a lot of places: papers on arXiv, YouTube explainers, long chats with AI assistants, Slack threads with my team, code I'm reading. I take notes in all of them. For a long time, those notes were in the wrong place.

The problem: notes that never come back

Two things kept happening.

My notes were cut off from where they came from. I'd write something while reading a paper or working in a chat, and it would go into a notes app somewhere else. The next time I opened that paper or that chat, the note didn't come with it. It was still there in a list, but nothing reminded me to look.

What I learned was scattered. Notes on a paper were in one place, notes on the video that explained it in another, and the AI chat where I worked through it in a third. I couldn't see what I'd learned over a week, and I couldn't see how the pieces connected.

I also wanted my notes to stay mine: on my Mac, searchable without sending them to a server.

The idea: a note lives where it's about

So I built NoteAnywhere. Every note belongs to a place: a Slack channel, a WhatsApp chat, a VS Code project, a web page. When you go back to that place, its notes open by themselves in a small floating panel.

It works in three steps:

  1. Write a note where you are. Press ⌥⇧M in any app, or ⌥⇧N in Chrome or Brave, and type.
  2. Leave. Switch chats, close the tab, get on with your day.
  3. Come back. Open that chat or page again and the note is waiting for you.

Every chat gets its own notes, and so does every web page: each YouTube video, each arXiv paper, each AI chat.

There's nothing to set up per app. NoteAnywhere reads the front window's title (with the macOS Accessibility permission) and turns it into a place: "Sam (DM) - Acme - Slack" becomes Slack › Acme › Sam (DM). A broader place sees the notes of the places under it, so Slack › Acme shows the notes of all its channels, and github.com shows the notes you wrote on its pages.

Connecting what I learned separately

The part I care about most: notes I wrote in different places, on different days, should find each other. Often the link between two things I learned is exactly what I didn't notice at the time.

  • Search by meaning, not just keywords. ⌥⇧Space opens a search box from anywhere. It finds notes by what they mean (a small embedding model, all-MiniLM-L6-v2, running on your Mac) as well as by exact words, so a note can turn up even when I don't remember the words I used.
  • A graph of how notes connect. Each note is joined to its app or site, the notes it links to, its #tags, and the notes with a similar meaning. The "similar" links are the ones that surprise me: two notes from a paper and a video a week apart, sitting next to each other.
  • Pages from a highlight. Highlight a question in a note and choose Add page: you get a new note titled with the question, linked both ways. Or Link to a note… to point it at an answer you already wrote.
  • #tags, nested. Type #ml/attention or #project/bugs anywhere. Search #ml and you get everything under it.

Tracking what I learned

I wanted to look back and see what I actually did. The calendar shows dated lines and tasks (type @fri or pick a date), and under each day, the notes you edited that day, each one as it was then. So "what did I learn last Tuesday?" has an answer.

Private by default

  • Notes are stored in a SQLite file on your Mac.
  • Search runs on your Mac. The ✨ writing actions use Apple's on-device model (macOS 26).
  • The browser extension only asks the app on your Mac whether a page has notes. It sends nothing to any server.
  • There are no analytics. Sync between devices is optional and end-to-end encrypted.

It's also light: about 0% CPU when idle and 37–65 MB of memory in my measurements.

Your AI assistant can read it too

NoteAnywhere is also an MCP server, so Claude, Codex or Gemini can search your notes, list your tasks for the week or add to a note, all running on your Mac. They can't delete notes or open locked ones.

Try it

Free and open source (GPL-3.0). The Mac app needs macOS 15 or later; the extension works in Chrome and Brave. An iPhone app is in progress, and an early Android app syncs with the Mac.
