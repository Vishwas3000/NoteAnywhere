I just open-sourced NoteAnywhere, a notes app for macOS where notes stay with the place they're about.

Write a note in a Slack channel, a VS Code project, a WhatsApp chat or on a web page. The next time you're back
there, the note is waiting in a small floating panel. No digging through folders, no searching for "that thing I wrote
about this client".

It knows where you are by reading the front window's title (with your permission, never message contents), and a
Chrome/Brave extension does the same for web pages.

Around that is a complete notes app: search by meaning with a model that runs on your Mac, Apple Intelligence
actions for grammar and checklists, #tags, a knowledge graph, a calendar for dated tasks, and a Markdown editor.

Two choices I care about:

• Local-first. Notes live in a database on your Mac and the AI runs on-device. Sync is optional and off by default.
• Open. It's free under GPL-3.0, and every design decision is written down in the repo with its reason.

I built it quickly with a lot of help from Claude Code. The interesting part wasn't the typing; it was deciding what
to build, measuring the options, and keeping the scope honest.
